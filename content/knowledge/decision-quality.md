Start an analytics or AI project by naming the decision it will change. Who makes that decision? How often? What information do they have today? What is the cost of acting too early, too late, or incorrectly?

## From model metric to decision metric

A model metric tells us how a system behaves on an evaluation set. A decision metric tells us whether that behavior helps in context. The bridge between them includes workflow timing, user trust, the ability to override, and the cost of different errors.

This is why a slightly less accurate model can create more value if it is faster, easier to explain, or better aligned with the real cost of false positives and false negatives.
